Lotus Foods

Consulting (via FEED) In progress

EPR packaging fee reporting tool

Problem
Lotus Foods sells rice in 7 states with new packaging laws (EPR) that charge brands for the packaging they sell. Every state has different rules, fees and deadlines, and working it out by hand is slow and easy to get wrong.
Approach
Our 8-person team researched each state’s law (I owned Oregon), then turned the rules into a spreadsheet tool. I built the Oregon fee calculation, which was integrated into the team’s MVP, and co-built a tab that applies Oregon’s packaging discounts and surcharges. Lotus enters its data once and sees what it owes in every state.
Result
The client found the MVP really useful and had no critiques. I found that switching Lotus’s rice pouches to single-layer plastic could cut its biggest Oregon fee by more than half, and I answered the client’s question on shelf-life risk live in the meeting.

NFL Big Data Bowl 2027

Personal Project In progress

Do Combine drills actually predict which receivers get open?

Problem
NFL teams judge wide receiver prospects partly on Combine agility drills, but a fast 3-cone time doesn’t always mean a player can get open in a game. Scouts need a clearer read on who can actually separate from defenders, and on which routes.
Approach
Using NFL player-tracking data, I’m measuring how receivers move through a cut (how fast they slow down, how much speed they keep and how quickly they re-accelerate) and testing whether those movements predict getting open better than drill times alone.
Result
Analysis in progress for the 2027 competition. Deliverable: a one-page route-runner profile scouts and coaches can read at a glance.

Butterfly-Inspired Solar Fruit Dryer

Class Project (IB 130) Building

A self-regulating solar dryer that turns surplus produce into shelf-stable food

Problem
Food banks, farmers markets and community gardens get large amounts of produce that’s close to spoiling, with no cold storage to keep it. Drying it in the open sun is slow and unsafe: rain, pests and overheating ruin the food.
Approach
I came up with the idea and designed how the system works. The enclosed dryer borrows three tricks from butterflies: a textured black panel to absorb heat, white reflective flaps to direct sunlight, and an Arduino that senses temperature and adjusts the flaps, vent and fan on its own. I co-led the technology: Arduino code, wiring and the CAD model.
Result
Physical build this week, demo next week. Next step: getting feedback from Berkeley Food Network.
