NFL Big Data Bowl 2027

Do Combine drills actually predict which receivers get open? In progress for the 2027 competition.

The question

The NFL Combine is the yearly event where college players run timed drills in front of teams before the draft. Do the movement skills receivers show there translate to getting open in real games, and do they tell teams more than the drill times already do?

Why it matters

Teams use Combine drills like the 3-cone drill and the shuttle run (short sprints with sharp turns) to judge a receiver’s agility, but those are just stopwatch times. They don’t show how a player changes direction, and there’s no defender. The NFL’s player-tracking data records every player’s position and speed many times a second during real games. If that data can show which movement skills lead to getting open, teams can spot draft prospects whose drill times undersell or oversell them.

Who it’s for

  • Scouts and evaluators: who’s better (or worse) than their drill times suggest
  • Coaches: the specific weakness to work on with a player, like a slow stop vs. a slow restart

My approach

1. Pick routes with one clear cut

A route is the path a receiver runs on a play, and a cut is a sharp change of direction. I chose four routes (slant, out, curl/hitch and dig) where the receiver runs forward and then makes one sharp cut, so it’s easy to isolate. I left out go routes (straight sprints down the field) because the 40-yard dash already measures straight-line speed.

2. Define “getting open”

Distance to the nearest defender at a set time after the cut and at the moment of the throw. Where the data allows, I adjust for the kind of defense he faced, where players lined up, and who was guarding him.

3. Measure three things about each cut

  • Slowing down: how fast the receiver brakes into the cut
  • Keeping speed: how much speed he carries out of the turn compared to going in
  • Getting back up to speed: how quickly he returns to near top speed after the cut

4. Test what drill times miss

Build two statistical predictions of how open a receiver gets: one using only Combine drill times, and one that also uses the cut measurements from tracking data. If the second predicts getting open better on each route, the movement data is telling teams something new.

What I’m building

A route-runner profile for each receiver: his cut measurements, how he ranks, and the routes where he’s projected to get open most.

What’s next

Build the measurements, run the comparison, and design the profile in time for the competition deadline.

Data

Player-tracking data from the NFL Big Data Bowl 2027 on Kaggle, a site that hosts data science competitions.
