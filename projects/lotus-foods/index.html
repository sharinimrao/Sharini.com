Case Study · Experience

Lotus Foods

EPR packaging fee reporting tool for Lotus Foods, via FEED @ Berkeley. In progress.

The client

Lotus Foods is a rice brand selling packaged products across the U.S. New “Extended Producer Responsibility” (EPR) laws act like a packaging tax: brands pay states based on how much packaging they sell and what it’s made of.

The problem

Seven states, seven different rulebooks. Each has its own eligibility thresholds, fee formulas, deadlines and penalties, which go up to $25,000/day in Oregon and $50,000/day in California. Lotus needed one repeatable way to report, not seven separate manual processes.

My role

Associate Consultant on an 8-person team. I owned Oregon end to end:

  • Research: eligibility, fee structure, penalties and fee-reduction opportunities, which I presented as part of our midterm deliverable
  • Oregon fee calculation: I built the tab that turns packaging data into an estimated Oregon fee. Its features were integrated into the team’s final MVP.
  • Eco-modulation tab (with a teammate): Oregon rewards better packaging with discounts and charges extra for worse packaging. We built a tab that applies those bonuses and penalties, so Lotus sees its real fee, not just the base rate, and can see what a packaging change would save.

What I found in Oregon

Oregon is already billing, and Lotus’s flexible plastic pouches fall into its most expensive fee category. I recommended three changes:

Switch pouch material

Under Oregon’s published rates, multi-layer plastic costs 102¢/lb versus 43¢/lb for single-layer film. That cuts Lotus’s biggest Oregon fee by more than half.

Lighten the packaging

Fees are charged by weight, so thinner film lowers costs right away. Every EPR state charges by weight, so this saves money everywhere, not just Oregon.

Push bigger bags

A 2 lb bag uses less packaging per pound of rice than two 1 lb bags, and it’s cheaper to produce and ship.

Presenting to the client

Lotus found the MVP really useful and had no critiques. Their one question was about risk: would switching from multi-layer to single-layer pouches hurt shelf life?

We’d already considered this. Multi-layer film protects against moisture and air, but dry goods like rice keep well in single-layer film. So we scoped the recommendation to Lotus’s dry-goods packaging, where the fee savings don’t come at the cost of product quality. I walked the client through that reasoning on the spot.

The tool

We translated the research into a spreadsheet with four parts:

  • Inputs: products, sales by state, packaging materials and fee rates, entered once
  • State calculations: each state’s rules applied to the same data
  • Summary: total estimated fees by state, a 2026 vs. 2027 comparison and a deadline tracker
  • Checks: flags mistakes like missing weights or unmatched products, plus plain-language formulas so Lotus’s team can trust the math

The eco-modulation tab turned the tool from a reporting tool into a decision tool: Lotus can test a packaging change, like switching pouch film, and see the fee impact before committing.

Key design goal: cut manual data entry for Lotus’s team.

What’s next

Testing the tool on Lotus’s real products, building scenario planning, and delivering a fee-reduction strategy and California source-reduction plan by end of semester.

What I learned

A recommendation is only as good as the risks you’ve already thought through. Cost savings mean nothing if the product spoils.
